**Why it works.** The joke is visible in one frame — a dog pulling at something that will not move —
and it escalates by *revealing*, not by repeating. Each tug exposes another foot of root, so the
audience keeps learning the problem is bigger than Tank thinks while Tank keeps concluding the
opposite. The ending lands because Tank is not beaten by a rival or a trick; he is beaten by
geology, which nobody can argue with.

## 2. WRITTEN STORY

Tank found the perfect stick.

It was the right length.
It was the right thickness.
One end came to a point, which Tank considered a feature.

He took hold of it.
It did not move.

Tank adjusted his footing and tried again.
It did not move again.

Pip arrived at a walk.
"Is it stuck?"

"It is seated," Tank said.

Mabel sat down where she could see all of it.

Tank pulled.
Two inches of stick came up.
Then four.
Then it stopped.

"Progress," Tank said.

Pip looked at the four inches.
Then at the ground on either side of them.
The dirt had lifted in a line, the way a carpet does when something underneath it moves.

"Tank."

"Working."

Pip followed the line with his nose.
It went past Tank.
It went past Mabel.
It went around the base of the tree and did not stop there.

"Tank, I do not think this is a stick."

"It is stick-shaped."

"So is a lot of a tree."

Tank pulled harder.
Another six inches came up, somewhere behind him this time, which was not where he was pulling.

Mabel watched the ground rise and settle three feet from where anyone was standing.

"It moved over there," she said.

"Then it is loosening."

"It is not loosening. It is long."

Pip came back from the tree.
"I followed it. It goes under the fence."

Tank stopped.
"Under?"

"Under."

Tank looked at the stick in his mouth.
Then at the fence.
Then at the distance between them, which was most of the yard.

Pip set a smaller stick down beside him.
A good one. Straight, dry, no attachments.

Tank looked at it the way you look at a replacement for something that was never going to be
replaced.

"That one is not seated."

"That is the point," Pip said.

Tank took one more pull.

The whole yard seemed to shift a half inch — the dirt by the tree, the dirt by the fence, the dirt
directly under Mabel, who stood up without comment and moved.

Everyone held still.

Tank let go.

The root settled back down with the quiet confidence of something that had been there first.

Tank sat.

"It is attached to the planet," he said.

"It is," said Mabel.

"I was losing to the planet."

"You were."

Tank considered this for a moment, and then he started laughing, in the way a very large dog laughs,
which is mostly in the shoulders.

Pip nudged the smaller stick closer.

Tank picked it up.
It came away from the ground instantly, with no resistance at all, and he looked faintly insulted
by how easy it was.

"This one is fine," he said.

He carried it three steps and dropped it.

Then he went back and looked at the root again.

"Tomorrow," he said.

Mabel followed him inside.

"Bring a shovel," she said.
