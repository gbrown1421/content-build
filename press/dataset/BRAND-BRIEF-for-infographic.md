# Design brief — RVA Cheap Flights press infographic

Use this as the visual specification. It is not data; it describes how the infographic must look.

## Brand

**RVA Cheap Flights** — a Richmond, Virginia flight-deal service tracking fares out of Richmond
International Airport (RIC).

## Logo

Use the supplied logo file `rva-logo-white.png`. It is a **white** logo, so it must sit on the
**navy** background — never on white or on a light panel. Place it top-left in the header.

## Color palette — LOCKED, use these and nothing else

| Role | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Navy** | `#153556` | Header background, headlines, and the bars for routes that carry a PREMIUM over normal |
| **Orange** | `#F37727` | Accent, and the bars for routes that are CHEAPER than normal |
| **White** | `#FFFFFF` | Page background and all text on navy |

Do not introduce any other colors. No gradients, no additional accent hues, no rainbow charts,
no teal, no purple, no red/green.

## How color carries meaning

This is a two-pole comparison and the colors are the key:

- **Orange = cheaper than that route's normal price floor.** Good for the traveler.
- **Navy = a premium over that route's normal floor.** Bad for the traveler.

Every bar must be directly labeled with its percentage, because orange on white is a low-contrast
pairing and the number must be readable without relying on the color alone.

## Typography and tone

Clean, editorial, newsroom-appropriate. This is going to journalists as supporting evidence, not
to consumers as an ad. No marketing exclamation marks, no stock photography of airplanes or
beaches, no clip art.

## The single message the infographic must land

**Whether Richmond is a bargain depends entirely on where you're going — and the split is not
what travelers expect.**

RIC is genuinely cheap to Charleston, Orlando, Tampa, Fort Myers and Nashville. It carries a
significant premium to Miami, Fort Lauderdale, Boston and the New York airports — the short-haul
routes people assume will be cheap. Miami and Fort Lauderdale are the headline contradiction:
same state as Orlando, opposite answer.

## Required elements

1. Navy header with the white logo and the headline.
2. A stat row: 254 fares graded · 64 days tracked · $52 cheapest fare · 23 top-grade fares.
3. **The main chart:** a horizontal diverging bar chart of each route's average fare versus that
   route's normal price floor. Orange bars extend right (cheaper), navy bars extend left
   (premium). Sorted best to worst. Every bar labeled with its percentage and route name.
4. A callout for Charleston reaching $98 on four separate dates: Sep 8, Sep 15, Sep 25, Oct 9.
5. A two-column "same state, opposite answer" comparison.
6. A footer carrying the method note, the caveats, and the contact line:
   **Glenn Brown · RVA Cheap Flights · rvacheapflights.com · 804-210-9657**

## Accuracy rules — do not deviate

- 254 graded fares, August 17 to October 19, 2026. Not 259.
- Charleston hit $98 **four** times, not five.
- Do not invent any figure that is not in the data source. No subscriber counts, no savings totals,
  no audience numbers, no percentages that aren't in the CSV.
